# Citations and sources

This page lists the works referred to in the text, with a one-line note on what each contributes. Only works the author is genuinely confident exist are named. No page numbers, DOIs, or specific study results are asserted as fact; every specific statistic in the book is flagged in an `:::uncertainty` block for verification before publishing.

## Working memory and attention (Chapter 1)

- **Baddeley, A. D., & Hitch, G. (1974). "Working Memory."** In G. H. Bower (ed.), *The Psychology of Learning and Motivation*. The origin of the influential multi-component model of working memory (verbal loop, visual sketchpad, central executive). Developed further in Baddeley's later overviews, e.g. *Working Memory, Thought, and Action* (2007).
- **Miller, G. A. (1956). "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two."** *Psychological Review*. The classic statement of a small, fixed limit on immediate memory. Cited here for the *shape* of the claim, not a precise capacity.
- **Cowan, N.** Work associated with the revision of immediate-memory capacity to roughly four "chunks" once rehearsal is controlled. Named as the source of a widely cited later figure; the exact number is left in an uncertainty block.
- **Sweller, J. (1988). "Cognitive Load During Problem Solving."** *Cognitive Science*. Origin of cognitive load theory and the intrinsic/extraneous distinction used throughout Section 1.3.

## How learning sticks (Chapter 2)

- **Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). "Test-Enhanced Learning."** *Psychological Science*. Central demonstration of the testing effect / retrieval practice, especially at longer retention intervals.
- **Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques."** *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*. The review that rates highlighting and re-reading low, and practice testing and distributed practice high.
- **Ebbinghaus, H. (1885). *Über das Gedächtnis*** (trans. *Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology*). The pioneering study of forgetting and the benefit of distributed practice.
- **Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006).** A quantitative synthesis of distributed-practice studies, named as the source of specific spacing figures — which are deliberately left unquoted and flagged for verification.
- **Bjork, R. A.** The concept of "desirable difficulties" — difficulties that lower present performance but improve durable learning and transfer. See Bjork's writing on memory and metamemory in training.
- **Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence."** *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*. The careful review finding no strong support for matching instruction to individual "learning styles."

## The tired brain (Chapter 3)

- **Diekelmann, S., & Born, J. (2010). "The Memory Function of Sleep."** *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*. Review casting sleep as an active contributor to memory consolidation.
- **Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.** Farrar, Straus and Giroux. The popular presentation of the System 1 / System 2 framing, explicit that the two "systems" are a useful shorthand.
- **Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974). "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases."** *Science*. The foundational paper on systematic biases arising from mental shortcuts.
- **Baumeister, R. F., and colleagues.** Work on "ego depletion," the usual theoretical basis for decision fatigue. Cited alongside the note that large replication efforts in the 2010s have questioned the effect; the mechanism and its specific figures are not asserted as established.

## Expertise and skill (Chapter 4)

- **Chase, W. G., & Simon, H. A. (1973). "Perception in Chess."** *Cognitive Psychology*. The classic reconstruction experiments locating chess mastery in learned patterns ("chunks") rather than general memory, building on Adriaan de Groot's earlier studies.
- **Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R. T., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993). "The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance."** *Psychological Review*. The founding statement of the deliberate-practice framework.
- **Ericsson, K. A., & Pool, R. (2016). *Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise*.** The accessible late account, including Ericsson's objections to the "10,000-hour rule" popularisation of his violin data.
- **Macnamara, B. N., Hambrick, D. Z., & Oswald, F. L. (2014). "Deliberate Practice and Performance in Music, Games, Sports, Education, and Professions: A Meta-Analysis."** *Psychological Science*. The meta-analytic challenge arguing deliberate practice explains a smaller share of performance differences than the strong programme implies. Cited as one side of a live dispute; its specific variance percentages are deliberately left unquoted and flagged for verification.
- **Thorndike, E. L.** Early twentieth-century work on transfer of training ("identical elements"), named as the origin of the near/far transfer distinction used in Section 4.5.

## The multilingual mind (Chapter 5)

- **Bialystok, E., Craik, F. I. M., & Luk, G. (2012). "Bilingualism: Consequences for Mind and Brain."** *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*. A representative statement of the bilingual-advantage research programme, cited as one side of a contested debate.
- **Paap, K. R., and colleagues.** The principal sceptical line on the bilingual advantage in the mid-2010s — replication failures and publication-bias analyses. Cited as the other side of the same open debate; no effect size on either side is asserted.
- **Kroll, J. F., and colleagues.** Experimental work on parallel activation and competition between a bilingual's languages, the basis of Section 5.1.
- **Gollan, T. H., and colleagues.** Research on tip-of-the-tongue states and lexical access in bilinguals, cited for the frequency-and-competition account in Section 5.1.
- **Keysar, B., Hayakawa, S. L., & An, S. G. (2012). "The Foreign-Language Effect: Thinking in a Foreign Tongue Reduces Decision Biases."** *Psychological Science*. The opening study of the foreign-language effect, presented as a promising but young and contested literature.
- **Krashen, S.** The input hypothesis and the notion of comprehensible input in second-language acquisition — cited with the note that the strong theoretical claim is contested while the practical core is widely accepted.

## Thinking together (Chapter 6)

- **Wegner, D. M. (1987). "Transactive Memory: A Contemporary Analysis of the Group Mind."** In B. Mullen & G. R. Goethals (eds.), *Theories of Group Behavior*. The origin of transactive memory; later extended by Wegner and colleagues to memory outsourced to search engines.
- **Janis, I. L. (1972). *Victims of Groupthink*** (revised as *Groupthink*, 1982). The case-study analysis of policy fiascos that introduced groupthink and its procedural remedies. Cited with the note that the packaged syndrome has replicated only partially, while its component mechanisms are well documented.
- **Surowiecki, J. (2004). *The Wisdom of Crowds*.** A journalist's synthesis, cited cautiously and chiefly for its statement of the failure conditions — lost independence, homogeneity, social influence. Galton's ox-weighing observation appeared in *Nature* (1907).
- **Diehl, M., & Stroebe, W. (1987). "Productivity Loss in Brainstorming Groups: Toward the Solution of a Riddle."** *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. The experimental isolation of production blocking as the main mechanism behind brainstorming's underperformance.
- **Woolley, A. W., Chabris, C. F., Pentland, A., Hashmi, N., & Malone, T. W. (2010). "Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups."** *Science*. The opening of the collective-intelligence programme, cited as young and partially contested; none of its specific correlations are asserted as fact.

## A note on verification

Bibliographic details above (years, journals, exact titles) are given from memory of well-known, canonical works and should be checked against a library catalogue or the original publications before this book is printed or otherwise reused. Where the text makes any quantitative claim — a capacity figure, a retention percentage, a spacing multiplier, an effect size — that claim is deliberately confined to an `:::uncertainty` block carrying the phrase "citation needed — verify before publishing", and must be sourced to a specific, verified study before it is stated as fact.
