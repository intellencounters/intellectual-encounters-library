# Citations

The works below are cited in the footnotes of this book. All are well-known,
canonical texts and thinkers; they are listed here so a reader can trace an idea
back to its source. Only works the author is genuinely confident exist are
included. No page numbers, edition specifics, or study results should be reused
from this list without checking them against the actual publications first — see
the verification note at the end.

## Chapter 1 — The Nervous System You Commute With

- **Robert M. Sapolsky, *Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers* (1994; revised 2004).** The
  standard popular-science account of stress physiology and its mismatch with
  chronic, modern, psychological stressors.
- **Richard S. Lazarus and Susan Folkman, *Stress, Appraisal, and Coping*
  (1984).** The foundational statement of the transactional (appraisal) model of
  stress, including primary and secondary appraisal.
- **Epictetus, *Enchiridion* (c. 125 CE).** The Stoic source for the idea that
  people are disturbed not by things but by their judgements of them — the
  ancient anticipation of cognitive appraisal.
- **Christina Maslach, *Burnout: The Cost of Caring* (1982); Christina Maslach
  and Michael P. Leiter, *The Truth About Burnout* (1997).** Established burnout
  as a three-dimensional syndrome driven by chronic worker–workplace mismatch.

## Chapter 2 — Why Motivation Fails and Habits Save You

- **Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan, *Intrinsic Motivation and
  Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (1985); Edward L. Deci, *Why We Do What
  We Do* (1995).** The core statements of self-determination theory.
- **Timothy A. Pychyl, *Solving the Procrastination Puzzle* (2013).** Frames
  procrastination as a failure of emotion regulation rather than time management;
  associated with research conducted with Fuschia Sirois.
- **William James, *The Principles of Psychology* (1890).** Chapter 4, "Habit,"
  is the classic account of habit as the "fly-wheel" of behaviour.
- **Charles Duhigg, *The Power of Habit* (2012).** Popularised the
  cue–routine–reward description of the habit loop.
- **Wendy Wood, *Good Habits, Bad Habits* (2019).** Research-based account of how
  stable context, rather than motivation, sustains habitual behaviour. (Cited in
  an evidence block rather than a footnote.)

## Chapter 3 — Who You Are When No One's Watching

- **Charles Horton Cooley, *Human Nature and the Social Order* (1902); George
  Herbert Mead, *Mind, Self, and Society* (1934).** Sources for the socially
  constituted self and the "looking-glass self."
- **Erik H. Erikson, *Childhood and Society* (1950); *Identity: Youth and Crisis*
  (1968).** Locate the formation of a stable identity as the central task of
  adolescence and early adulthood.
- **John Bowlby, *Attachment* (1969), with Mary Ainsworth's "Strange Situation"
  research; Cindy Hazan and Phillip Shaver, "Romantic Love Conceptualized as an
  Attachment Process" (1987).** Founded attachment theory and extended it to
  adult romantic relationships. (Hazan and Shaver are cited in an evidence block.)
- **Roy F. Baumeister and Mark R. Leary, "The Need to Belong" (1995).** Argues
  that the drive to form and maintain stable close relationships is a fundamental
  human motivation.
- **John T. Cacioppo and William Patrick, *Loneliness* (2008).** Reframes
  loneliness as an evolved biological signal that can bias social perception.

## Chapter 4 — Working With Feeling: Emotion as Information

- **Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made* (2017).** The constructed-emotion
  account, including emotional granularity and its association with regulation.
- **James J. Gross (ed.), *Handbook of Emotion Regulation*.** The standard
  research synthesis of Gross's process model, distinguishing antecedent-focused
  strategies (reappraisal) from response-focused ones (suppression); the
  comparative findings draw on Gross's experimental work, including studies with
  Oliver John.
- **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* (c. 340 BCE).** Book IV on anger felt at the
  right things, with the right people, in the right way and measure.
- **June Price Tangney and Ronda L. Dearing, *Shame and Guilt* (2002).** The
  standard research treatment of guilt as act-focused and repair-oriented versus
  shame as self-focused and linked to hiding.

## Chapter 5 — Two People, One Conversation

- **John M. Gottman and Nan Silver, *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage
  Work* (1999); John M. Gottman, *What Predicts Divorce?* (1994).** The
  observational research on couples: complaint versus criticism, the "four
  horsemen," flooding, and repair attempts. The headline prediction-accuracy
  figures associated with this work are methodologically contested and are
  flagged in an uncertainty block rather than asserted.
- **Carl R. Rogers, *On Becoming a Person* (1961).** Empathic understanding —
  grasping the other's meaning from within their frame — as a core condition of
  helpful communication.
- **Irwin Altman and Dalmas A. Taylor, *Social Penetration: The Development of
  Interpersonal Relationships* (1973).** The classic account of intimacy as
  gradual, reciprocal deepening of self-disclosure.

## Chapter 6 — Can People Change?

- **Brent W. Roberts and colleagues.** The programme of longitudinal research and
  meta-analyses on personality trait development across adulthood: rank-order
  stability, mean-level change, the maturity principle, and social investment.
- **Albert Bandura, *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* (1997).** The
  comprehensive statement of self-efficacy theory and the ordering of its
  sources, with mastery experiences first.
- **Carol S. Dweck, *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success* (2006).** The
  popular statement of fixed versus growth mindsets; the subsequent replication
  debate (large pre-registered trials with collaborators including David Yeager,
  and critical meta-analyses) is presented in the chapter with both sides
  steel-manned.
- **Ann S. Masten, *Ordinary Magic: Resilience in Development* (2014).**
  Resilience as the ordinary operation of basic adaptive systems rather than rare
  individual steel.
- **George A. Bonanno, *The Other Side of Sadness* (2009).** Research on
  bereavement and potential trauma showing stable functioning to be the most
  common trajectory.
- **Richard G. Tedeschi and Lawrence G. Calhoun, work on post-traumatic growth
  (from the mid-1990s).** Established the concept and its measurement; whether
  reported growth reflects actual change remains a recognised open question.

## A note on verification

This book is written for general education, not as clinical or medical guidance,
and nothing in it should be used to diagnose a person or to replace professional
help. Where a specific statistic or empirical claim could not be responsibly
vouched for, it has been placed in an uncertainty block rather than asserted, and
marked with the phrase "citation needed — verify before publishing." Titles and
dates above are given from memory of well-known works; before any of these
references is quoted, given a page number, or otherwise relied upon in a
published or graded context, it should be checked against the actual publication.
